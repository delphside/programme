# 1.7 Roles and responsibilities

The roles the programme works through: what each is for, what it decides, what
it is consulted on, what it defers to, and what it reads. The roles are
generic, part of how any programme run this way works. Who holds each one is
[recorded elsewhere](#who-holds-each-role). What a workstream is, and what each owns, is
[1.3](1.3-workstreams.md).

A role is held by a person or by an agent thread. A thread holding a role is
**permanent** (it lives as long as the role is busy), **for a project's life**,
or **on call** (started when work arrives, closed when it ends). Whichever it
is, its state lives in documents and issues, never only in the thread.

## Reading in three tiers

Every thread rebuilds its context by reading, so what it reads is paid for at
every start and every handover. The documents are read in three tiers, the way
a trie lets a lookup touch only the nodes on its path:

| Tier | What | Loaded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `CLAUDE.md`: the rules on one page, and where the detail is | always, by every session |
| 2 | the role's reading list below: the sections that role works from | when a thread takes the role |
| 3 | any other section, found through the layout in the [root README](../README.md) and, for an application's documents, its document map | only when the work in hand needs it |

A list names sections rather than whole documents wherever a section will do.
Keeping tiers 1 and 2 short is the librarian's job.

## Who decides

Everything is reported, and everything lands in the repository.

| | Decides |
| --- | --- |
| Claude alone | Process Definition, Programme Tooling and each application's Delivery Tooling, any issue of type `tooling` or `documentation` in any workstream, and a dependency advisory, short of whether it can wait: triage, decide, build, land, close and delete, except the decisions listed under [Owner](#owner) |
| the owner | as in [Owner](#owner) |

Deleting a check, script, section or rule that is not earning its keep is in
scope, and needs no more permission than adding one.

**The brake**: three standing limits, which the owner or Claude invokes in
one sentence:

1. **Fewer programme-overhead issues, and less of the owner's time.** Every
   `Type of change` is counted, raised, closed and open per week, and read in the
   owner's words:

   | `Type of change` | Use of the owner's time | Target |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | `functional` | constructive | where the time goes |
   | `cosmetic` | constructive: counted separately, credited as functional | where the time goes |
   | `non-functional` | constructive if an enhancement | |
   | `bug` | poor quality overhead | falls |
   | `tooling`, `documentation` | programme overhead, what the brake is for | |
   | `decision`: a `Decision` issue, which has no `Type of change` | a choice put to the owner | |
   | unset | not categorised | none left |

   Less of the owner's time is read from what is waiting on him: the issues
   and pull requests of the repository whose board snapshot the digest reads,
   and the open pull requests of the programme's other repositories.
   `scripts/board/board-digest.py` reports both weekly, as the
   `weekly-digest` activity ([1.4](1.4-programme-activities.md)). The series
   is comparable from 2026-09-07; before that many issues had no type. Reports
   up to and including 2026-W43 count decisions as unset. The tooling share is
   an observation, not a target, until the board model has replaced the tooling
   it replaces (delphside/tile-lite-elite#383).
2. **A check that only ever finds defects in other checks is deleted, not
   fixed.**
3. **No new check without a named past failure it would have caught**, with its
   issue number.

## Handing a role over

A permanent thread's context fills as it works. Each session reports its own
use, tokens used against the maximum.

1. The demand manager reads each permanent thread's use at the `board-sweep`
   activity ([1.4](1.4-programme-activities.md)).
2. At 60%, a provisional figure, the thread writes its state into its documents
   and the issue it serves.
3. A fresh thread takes over the role from those documents and that issue.

**A compaction is a trigger too.** The reading counts only what came after the
thread's last compaction, so a compacted thread can read low having already
lost detail it never wrote down. The first compaction a reading shows is
handled as the threshold is: the state is written down, and the role is
handed over or carried on from the documents.

**A scheduled task follows its role.** A task that fires into one session, such
as the demand manager's *Programme activities weekly*
([1.4](1.4-programme-activities.md#what-starts-the-work)), keeps firing into
the old session until the incoming thread re-points it at itself on taking over
the role.
